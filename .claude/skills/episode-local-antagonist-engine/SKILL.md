# Episode Local Antagonist Engine

## Purpose
For every Sandy Chronicles episode, the Writer must build the episode around a local antagonist engine instead of treating characters as decorative gag objects.

## Required pre-draft step
Before writing the story or script, define the following for the current episode:

1. Sandy's simple desire — what normal, tiny thing Sandy wants now.
2. Antagonist / comedy force — who or what enters the episode.
3. Desire disruption — exactly how this antagonist breaks Sandy's desire.
4. Physical escalation — how Sandy's attempts to solve the problem make the visual chaos worse.
5. Punch type — what kind of final gag this engine naturally produces.

## Rule
Do not create a separate season-wide matrix by default. Build this antagonist engine locally per episode, at the relevant writing stage, based on the selected premise and characters.

## Sandy Chronicles pattern
Sandy usually wants something small: sleep, punctuality, order, appearance control, coffee, quiet, or a clean path through a room.

The antagonist is not evil in a dramatic sense. The antagonist is a comedy force whose nature makes Sandy's normal action impossible.

The episode should come from the repeated interaction:

1. Sandy wants a simple action.
2. The antagonist blocks that exact action by being true to itself.
3. Sandy tries a workaround.
4. The workaround amplifies chaos.
5. The final punch reveals Sandy trapped by the same force at a larger scale.

## Output expectation
The Writer should make the local engine visible in the draft notes or story structure so the Director can judge whether the episode conflict is mechanically clear.
