## 0. TL;DR (act on this now)

1. **Shorts are the engine, long-form is the library.** Cut a 15–30s vertical Short from the funniest beat of every episode; that Short is what a 0-sub channel gets discovered on.
2. **Silence is the moat.** No dialogue = no translation, no language gate = the whole planet is the audience. Lead with it everywhere.
3. **Publish rhythm, not a dump.** 1 Short every 2–3 days as the funnel; 1 full episode/week as the payoff. Don't unlist-flip all 10 at once.
4. **One recognizable face.** Every thumbnail = Sandy's glass body + one big expression + the episode's key object, same lockup every time.
5. **Category Comedy, "Made for Kids = NO" (see §8), default upload = Unlisted → schedule Public.**

## 1. Positioning & Channel Promise

**One-line hook:** *A little glass hourglass tries to survive a heavy world — and gravity always wins.*

**Who it's for:** anyone, anywhere, any age, any language. Fans of silent physical comedy (Pink Panther, Mr. Bean, Larva, Shaun the Sheep, La Linea). People scrolling for a 20-second laugh that needs zero setup.

**Why silent = global (say this out loud in the copy):**

- Watch-time is universal — a physical gag holds attention without subtitles, so retention (the metric that actually drives the algorithm) stays high across every geo.

## 2. Shorts-First Growth Strategy (the funnel)

A 0-sub channel cannot grow on 90-second horizontal videos alone — nobody browses to an unknown channel. **Shorts are the top of the funnel; full episodes are the destination.**

```
Shorts (discovery)  →  channel visit / subscribe  →  Full episodes (session time)  →  Playlist binge  →  loyal sub
```

### Cadence
| Asset | Frequency | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical Short (15–30s) | every 2–3 days | discovery / reach / sub-driver |
| Full horizontal episode | 1× per week | payoff / watch-time / library depth |

One episode yields **1 full-length upload + 1–3 Shorts** (different beats), so ~10 episodes = weeks of Shorts inventory before we ever run dry.

### How to cut a Short from an episode
1. **Find the single funniest self-contained beat** — one gag with a clear setup→escalation→punch inside 15–30s. It must read with zero prior context.
2. **Reframe to 9:16 vertical.** Sandy stays centered; crop to keep the glass body + the key object in frame. For the already-vertical Airport episode, the Short is native — cut a 20s slice.
3. **Front-load the hook:** the first 1–2 seconds must show the absurd situation (Sandy vs the treadmill, Sandy vs the vending machine). No slow intro — the thumbnail-in-motion IS the hook.
4. **Loop it.** End the Short on a beat that flows back into the opening frame so it auto-replays — replay counts are watch-time.
5. **Caption-free but caption a title card** if the gag needs a 3-word frame ("Sandy vs. Gravity"). Optional, sparing.

### `#Shorts` usage
- Put **`#Shorts`** in the title OR the first line of the description (title placement is strongest). Keep it plus 2–3 topical tags: `#Shorts #silentcomedy #animation`.
- Vertical, ≤60s (we target 15–30s), and it auto-enters the Shorts feed.
- Every Short's description links/CTAs to the **full episode** ("▶ Watch the full episode: <link>") — that's the funnel handoff.

## 3. Title Formula

**Structure:** `Hook (the situation, visual + funny) + | + Keyword tail (silent / cartoon / animation)` — keep **≤ 100 characters** (YouTube truncates ~60–70 in feed, so front-load).

**SEO keywords to rotate into the tail:** `silent comedy`, `no dialogue`, `funny cartoon`, `funny animation`, `animated short`, `physical comedy`, `2D animation`.

**Rules:**
- Hook first, keyword second — the searchable word must survive truncation but the human-grabbing bit comes first.
- Name the concrete object/situation (people search "vending machine cartoon", not "episode 5").
- No clickbait we can't pay off. Curiosity, not lies.

## 6. Thumbnail Language

Owned by **EXEC-THUMB**; apply the `viral-thumbnail-design` skill. Shorts don't use a custom thumbnail (the feed uses a frame), so thumbnails matter most for **full episodes and the browse/search grid**.

**What makes a Sandy thumbnail instantly recognizable (the lockup):**
1. **Sandy's glass hourglass body, large and centered/left** — the silhouette IS the brand. Never crop him out or shrink him.
2. **One big readable expression** — panic, dread, false triumph. Emotion sells the click; a flat face doesn't.
3. **The episode's key object, large and obvious** — the vending machine, the treadmill, the phone, the fan. The viewer should decode the premise in <1 second.
4. **High contrast + bold background color** — a single saturated field (not a busy scene) so the thumbnail pops in a crowded grid and at phone size. Rotate the background hue per episode for shelf variety, keep everything else constant.
5. **Minimal or no text.** Silent comedy sells visually. If a word helps, ≤ 2–3 huge words ("SNACK TIME?"), heavy outline, high contrast — readable at 120px.

**Consistency rules (so the channel page reads as one series):**
- Same character rendering, same outline weight, same lighting every time.
- Same corner treatment / optional tiny ⏳ mark for brand continuity.
- Expression + object change per episode; everything else is the template.
- Test at mobile size (the majority of views) — if Sandy and the object aren't clear at thumbnail-scale, redo it.

**Viral-thumbnail-design principles to enforce:** one focal subject, emotion-forward face, strong figure/ground contrast, rule-of-thirds placement, no clutter, legible at the smallest render size.

## 7. Playlists, Cards & End Screens

These raise **session time** (consecutive views), YouTube's strongest growth lever.

**Playlists:**
- **"Sandy the Hourglass — Full Episodes"** — all horizontal episodes, chronological. This is the binge surface; set it as a featured playlist on the channel home.
- **"Sandy Shorts"** — all vertical cuts (auto-groups Shorts too).
- Optional themed playlist later ("Sandy vs. Machines") once the catalog justifies it.
- Playlists rank in search themselves — give them keyword-rich titles + descriptions.

**Cards (in-video, full episodes):**
- Add a **"next episode" card** near the end of each full episode pointing to the next one (or the playlist). Keeps the viewer inside the channel.
- Optional mid-video card only if there's a natural lull — usually skip; silent comedy shouldn't be interrupted.

**End screens (last 5–20s, full episodes only — not Shorts):**
- Element 1: **Subscribe** button.
- Element 2: **Next / best-performing video** (or "latest upload").
- Leave the animation's final punch clean; place the end-screen overlay so it doesn't cover the last gag frame — extend a hold if needed.

## 8. Settings Defaults

| Setting | Default | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Category** | **Comedy** | correct genre bucket for all episodes |
| **Video language** | **English** (metadata) with **"None / no spoken dialogue"** where offered | audio has no speech; keep title/description language = English for the widest metadata index |
| **Made for Kids (COPPA)** | **NO — "Not made for kids"** (recommended default) | see tradeoff below |
| **Default privacy (rollout)** | **Unlisted → scheduled Public** | upload/prep unlisted, flip to Public on the cadence (§9) |
| **Comments** | On, held-for-review or default | community fuels the algorithm; monitor for spam |
| **License** | Standard YouTube | — |
| **Allow embedding** | On | free distribution |
| **Shorts remixing** | On | lets others remix = free reach |

**Made-for-Kids tradeoff (state it, decide it):**
- Marking **"Made for Kids"** *disables* comments, notifications, end screens, cards, and personalized ads on the video — it **guts the exact growth tools** in §7 and kills the subscribe funnel. It's legally required only if the content is *directed to children* as the primary audience.
- Sandy is **family-friendly all-ages silent comedy**, not children's programming aimed primarily at under-13s (à la nursery-rhyme/toy channels). The honest, defensible classification is **"Not made for kids"**, set at the video level (audience: general).
- **Recommendation: default NO (not made for kids).** This keeps comments, cards, end screens, and notifications — essential for a 0-sub channel — while the content stays clean and all-ages. If the Director ever pivots to a preschool-targeted push, revisit per-video. (This is a classification judgment; if in doubt on a specific episode, the Director makes the call.)

## 9. Publishing Cadence & Rollout Plan

**Current state:** ~10 episodes uploaded **Unlisted**. Goal: convert to a live, growing public channel without dumping everything at once (a dump gives the algorithm no rhythm and buries older episodes instantly).

**Phase 0 — Channel prep (once):**
- Confirm branding: banner, avatar, About = the §1 promise line, links, featured playlist set to "Full Episodes."
- Build both playlists (§7) from the unlisted videos while still unlisted.
- Have EXEC-COPY + EXEC-THUMB finish titles/descriptions/tags/thumbnails for the **first 3–4 episodes** before go-live.

**Phase 1 — Launch (week 1):**
- Pick the **strongest, most universal episode** as the flagship first public video (candidates: Vending Machine or Gym — instantly legible premise, strong escalation). Flip it **Public**.
- Same day, publish its **Short** to start the funnel.

**Phase 2 — Steady rhythm (ongoing):**
- **1 full episode → Public per week** (best remaining episode next; save weaker ones for mid-catalog).
- **1 Short every 2–3 days**, drawn from the current + upcoming episodes.
- **Schedule Public** (don't manual-flip) at a consistent day/time so the audience and algorithm learn the pattern. Pick a slot and hold it.
- Each new full episode gets its Short(s) released around the same window to cross-feed.

**Ordering principle:** front-load your 2–3 best for momentum, then alternate strong/medium to keep the channel average high — never publish three weak ones in a row early.

**Promotion rule:** every Short description points to the matching full episode; every full-episode end screen points to the next episode + subscribe. The loop is self-reinforcing.
